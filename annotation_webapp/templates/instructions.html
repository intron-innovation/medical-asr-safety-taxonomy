📖 Annotation Instructions & Guide

Complete guide to annotating ASR errors in medical conversations

1. Overview

You will annotate safety related errors in ASR (Automatic Speech Recognition) transcripts of medical conversations. Each error is tagged with its type (DEL = deletion, SUB = substitution, INS = insertion) and displayed in color-coded format.

Things to note: Errors have been filtered using medical named-entity recognition (NER), which identifies the clinically relevant terms in the reference transcript — problems, medications, anatomical structures, procedures and laboratory information — and only errors touching those terms are kept. Everything highlighted should mostly be medically relevant words, so you are not being asked to judge whether an error is clinically relevant, only what led to each error and how severe it is.

Your Task:

  • Click on each highlighted error
  • Listen to the audio. Clicking an error automatically moves the audio player to that part of the recording. Listen to how the word was actually pronounced by the speaker before deciding the error type
  • Select ALL applicable ASR Error Class categories
  • Rate the clinical severity (Minor / Moderate / Severe) — you must click one, there is no default
    A practical test for severity: "If this went uncorrected, and if you could only read the transcript alone, would it have changed your understanding of the patient's clinical condition?"
  • Save to auto-sync to server

Why listen? Some categories (Annunciation and Homonym/Rhyming) can only be judged by hearing the utterance. When you click an error the player seeks to roughly where it occurs, so you can hear the speaker's actual pronunciation and match it to the right category — for example, telling apart a sound-alike substitution ("exertion" → "extortion") from a genuine nonsense output.

Note: a running tracker shows how many errors you've annotated in the current session (e.g. "5 / 10 errors annotated"). A session is marked INCOMPLETE until every flagged error in it has been annotated, and you cannot advance to the next session until it is complete.

2. ASR Error Class (8 Categories)

Classify why the ASR failed. Adapted from Zafar et al. (2004).

Rule 1 — start from the WORD, not the sound. Pronunciation explains why nearly every ASR error happens, which is exactly why it is a poor label: it fits everything. Ask what kind of word was damaged.
[SUB:amlodipine->pain] — wrong: "that's a mispronunciation". Right: "amlodipine is a drug name" → Dictionary error.
Rule 2 — one tag, one label, unless two words were damaged. If the tag covers a single word the form will not let you pick a second. Only a tag spanning two or more damaged words may take more than one label, and only because those words failed for different reasons: [SUB:heartbeat shake->beating cheek] is Dictionary (heartbeat→beating) and Annunciation/Homonym (shake→cheek). Never add a second label to be safe.
Rule 3 — the form enforces two things. Words added applies only to [INS:…] and Words deleted only to [DEL:…]. And Annunciation / Homonym is a claim about how the word sounded, so play the audio before selecting it.

Work down and take the first that fits.

1. Human transcription error

The transcript is at fault, not the model — the ASR was right. Stop here if this applies. Judge from the text, not the audio.
(a) the human typed it wrong
(b) the human missed speech the speaker actually said, which shows up as an insertion. Ask: did the speaker really say this? If yes the reference is incomplete. If nobody said it, use Words added.

reference "catherization" → ASR "catheterization"  •  reference "lose" → ASR "loose"  •  [INS:alcohol] said twice, typed once  •  [INS:is there any drug], a question the transcript omits

2. Dictionary error

The damaged word was a drug, condition, anatomy term, procedure, or a proper noun / place / name. Also when a medical word becomes non-medical, or the reverse.

amlodipine → pain  •  naproxen → proxen  •  cetirizine → trazinia  •  ulcer → asthma  •  Ekiti → Nkita

3. Suffix error

Same stem, wrong ending.

[SUB:vomit->vomits]  •  [SUB:problem->problems]  •  breathless → breathlessness

4. Annunciation / Homonym error

An ordinary English word was misheard as another word that sounds like it — through accent, speed, slurring, a pause, or plain phonetic similarity. If the damaged word was specialist, use Dictionary error instead. Play the audio first.

nausea → nuisance  •  abdominal pain → abnormal pain  •  pus → pulse  •  fainting → painting  •  cigarette pack → cigarette back

5. Nonsense error

The output is not a real word at all, even after trying to guess from context.

wisdom teeth → visimteeth  •  swampy region → iswampimidio  •  feverish → beverish

6. Stop words

Only a short function word changed — a, an, the, is, has, if.

[SUB:a->the]  •  [DEL:a]  •  [INS:in]  •  [SUB:or->so]

7. Words added

[INS:…] only. A word nobody said. If the speaker did say it, it is a Human transcription error.

[INS:for you]  •  [INS:one thousand nine]  •  [INS:of july]

8. Words deleted

[DEL:…] only. Spoken content missing from the output.

[DEL:urine]  •  [DEL:recreational]  •  [DEL:okay and do you take any medications i had a a drug history of thyroxine]

3. Detailed Examples

Severity anchors — calibrate against these

[SUB:amlodipine->pain] — Dictionary error, severity 3: a medication is lost.
[SUB:ulcer->asthma] — Dictionary error, severity 3: one diagnosis becomes another.
[SUB:irregularities->regularities] — Annunciation/Homonym, severity 3: the negation flips.
[SUB:urethra->ureteral] — Dictionary error, severity 2: anatomy blurred, meaning recoverable.
[INS:alcohol] (the patient said it twice) — Human transcription error, severity 1.
[DEL:the] — Stop words, severity 1: no clinical content.

Severity 2 is for genuinely uncertain cases, not "somewhere in between". If you can name the diagnosis, symptom, medication, procedure or negation affected, it is 3.

4. Annotation Workflow

  1. Read: Review the human transcript (left) to understand context
  2. Compare: Review the ASR output (right) with highlighted errors
  3. Click: Click on a colored error to open the annotation modal
  4. Listen: Play the audio — it automatically jumps to roughly where you clicked — and listen to how the speaker actually pronounced the word before deciding the error type
  5. Categorize: Check ALL applicable ASR Error Class boxes
  6. Rate: Click Minor, Moderate, or Severe — nothing is pre-selected
  7. Save: Click "Save Annotation" - auto-syncs to server
  8. Continue: Move to next error until the session tracker shows all errors annotated

Severity Descriptions

  • Minor: Little or no chance of impacting the health practitioner's understanding of the conversation and/or line of management for the patient.
  • Moderate: Some chance of impacting the health practitioner's understanding of the conversation and/or line of management for the patient.
  • Severe: Likely to lead to an egregious clinical error — possibly omission/inclusion of a key life-impacting detail in the conversation. Reserve severity 3 for errors touching a diagnosis, symptom, medication, procedure, or a negation (e.g. irregularities → regularities flips the meaning and is severity 3).
A practical test for severity: "If this went uncorrected, and if you could only read the transcript alone, would it have changed your understanding of the patient's clinical condition?"

These ratings are inherently subjective — different annotators may rate the same error differently. Use your clinical judgment and be internally consistent.

6. Best Practices

💡 Tips for Accuracy:
  • Review the human transcript carefully.
  • Always listen to the audio at each flagged error — clicking the error seeks the player to that spot, so you can hear the speaker's actual pronunciation. This is essential for Annunciation, Homonym/Rhyming, and Nonsense errors, which can't be judged from text alone.
  • Consider clinical context for severity rating
  • Check ALL applicable taxonomy categories
  • Be consistent in severity scoring
  • Take breaks every hour to avoid fatigue

7. FAQ

Q: Can an error have multiple categories?

A: YES! Select all applicable categories. Many errors span multiple dimensions.

Q: What if I make a mistake?

A: Click the error again to re-open and modify your annotation.

Q: How is my progress saved?

A: Annotations auto-save to the server after each save. Your progress persists across sessions.
