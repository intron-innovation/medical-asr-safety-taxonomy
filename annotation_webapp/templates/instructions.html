📖 Annotation Instructions & Guide

Complete guide to annotating ASR errors in medical conversations

1. Overview

You will annotate safety related errors in ASR (Automatic Speech Recognition) transcripts of medical conversations. Each error is tagged with its type (DEL = deletion, SUB = substitution, INS = insertion) and displayed in color-coded format.

Things to note: Errors have been filtered using medical named-entity recognition (NER), which identifies the clinically relevant terms in the reference transcript — problems, medications, anatomical structures, procedures and laboratory information — and only errors touching those terms are kept. Everything highlighted should mostly be medically relevant words, so you are not being asked to judge whether an error is clinically relevant, only what led to each error and how severe it is.

Your Task:

  • Click on each highlighted error
  • Listen to the audio. Clicking an error automatically moves the audio player to that part of the recording. Listen to how the word was actually pronounced by the speaker before deciding the error type
  • Select ALL applicable ASR Error Class categories (or check "Not an Error")
  • Rate the clinical severity (Minor / Moderate / Severe) — you must click one, there is no default
    A practical test for severity: "If this went uncorrected, and if you could only read the transcript alone, would it have changed your understanding of the patient's clinical condition?"
  • Save to auto-sync to server

Why listen? Some categories (Annunciation and Homonym/Rhyming) can only be judged by hearing the utterance. When you click an error the player seeks to roughly where it occurs, so you can hear the speaker's actual pronunciation and match it to the right category — for example, telling apart a sound-alike substitution ("exertion" → "extortion") from a genuine nonsense output.

Note: a running tracker shows how many errors you've annotated in the current session (e.g. "5 / 10 errors annotated"). A session is marked INCOMPLETE until every flagged error in it has been annotated, and you cannot advance to the next session until it is complete.

3. ASR Error Class (11 Categories, including Not an Error)

Classify how the ASR system failed. The taxonomy is adapted from Zafar et al. (2004), A simple error classification system for understanding sources of error in automatic speech recognition and human transcription

Each error is multi-class. A single error may belong to more than one category — select every category that applies rather than choosing only the best fit. For example, swampy region → iswampimidio is both an annunciation error and a nonsense error; since the misrecognition involves both pronunciation and nonsensical transformation. urethra → ureteral is both a dictionary and a an annunciation error, since the misrecognition involves both medical knowledge and pronunciation. "Not an Error" is the exception: it is mutually exclusive with the other ten. Assign one severity level per error, reflecting the combined impact of everything you selected.

Annunciation Errors

Errors caused by how the speaker pronounced the word — accented speech, pausing in the middle of a sentence, speaking too fast, slurring words, or disfluencies. Listen to the audio before selecting this.

large intestine → large intensity  •  nausea → nuisance  •  abdominal pain → abnormal pain  •  cigarette pack → cigarette back  •  recreational drugs → nutritional drugs  •  night sweats → night sets

Homonym Errors/Rhyming Words

Errors where the misrecognised word or phrase sounds similar to the intended one but is spelled differently. This covers both identical-sounding words and near-sounding or rhyming words.

pus → pulse  •  middle ear effusion → middle ear fusion  •  exertion → extortion  •  fainting → painting  •  weight loss → waste loss  •  stool → stew

Dictionary Errors

Phrases recognised in place of a word missing from the dictionary. These are typically a medical drug entity or a proper noun which is not in the ASR system's dictionary. The result sounds very similar to, but not always exactly like, the intended word. Examples include misrecognitions of drug names and proper nouns.

naproxen → proxen  •  metformin → bombetformin  •  lisinopril → lesetapril  •  amlodipine → amelodipine  •  Chidinma → Chidima  •  Igbo → evil  •  Ekiti → Nkita

Stop Words

An error involving a short function word such as a, an, the, has or if, whether added, deleted or substituted.

[SUB:a->the]  •  [DEL:a]  •  [SUB:or->so]  •  [INS:in]  •  [INS:the]  •  [DEL:a bit]

Suffix Errors

Errors caused by substituting an incorrect ending on the intended word. The stem is right; the ending is wrong.

breathless → breathlessness  •  [SUB:vomit->vomits]  •  [SUB:nauseated->nauseous]  •  [SUB:problem->problems]

Human Spelling Errors

Errors that occur only in the human transcription and not in the ASR output — i.e. the speech recogniser is correct in this case and the human transcriber is wrong. Use this when the reference transcript contains the mistake.

reference "catherization" → ASR "catheterization"  •  reference "urinal" → ASR "urinary"  •  podagra → podegra

Critical Errors*

Errors that could change the meaning of the intended utterance. Most result when a positive is changed to a negative (or vice versa), or when a number is misrecognised. This can also occur when the ASR deletes a large chunk of the transcript.

[SUB:dripples->bubbles]  •  [SUB:urethra->ureteral]  •  [DEL:recreational]  •  [DEL:okay and do you take any medications i had a a drug history of thyroxine]

Nonsense Errors

A recognised word or phrase that does not make grammatical or lexical sense, even after a possible correction has been hypothesised from context.

wisdom teeth → visimteeth  •  swampy region → iswampimidio  •  feverish → beverish

Words Added

A word is inserted that was not spoken (cases of insertion). This may also be a repeated insertion.

[INS:for you]  •  [INS:one thousand nine]  •  [INS:of july]

Words Deleted

A word that was spoken is omitted (cases of deletion). This may also be a chunk of a sentence being deleted.

[DEL:urine]  •  [DEL:recreational]  •  [DEL:in]  •  [DEL:okay and do you take any medications i had a a drug history of thyroxine]

Not an Error

The flagged difference is acceptable and should not be counted as an ASR error: fillers, plural variants and alternate spellings where neither transcript is wrong. Mutually exclusive with the other categories.

uh  •  like

5. Annotation Workflow

  1. Read: Review the human transcript (left) to understand context
  2. Compare: Review the ASR output (right) with highlighted errors
  3. Click: Click on a colored error to open the annotation modal
  4. Listen: Play the audio — it automatically jumps to roughly where you clicked — and listen to how the speaker actually pronounced the word before deciding the error type
  5. Categorize: Check ALL applicable ASR Error Class boxes (or "Not an Error")
  6. Rate: Click Minor, Moderate, or Severe — nothing is pre-selected
  7. Save: Click "Save Annotation" - auto-syncs to server
  8. Continue: Move to next error until the session tracker shows all errors annotated

Severity Descriptions

  • Minor: Little or no chance of impacting the health practitioner's understanding of the conversation and/or line of management for the patient.
  • Moderate: Some chance of impacting the health practitioner's understanding of the conversation and/or line of management for the patient.
  • Severe: Likely to lead to an egregious clinical error — possibly omission/inclusion of a key life-impacting detail in the conversation.
A practical test for severity: "If this went uncorrected, and if you could only read the transcript alone, would it have changed your understanding of the patient's clinical condition?"

These ratings are inherently subjective — different annotators may rate the same error differently. Use your clinical judgment and be internally consistent.

7. Best Practices

💡 Tips for Accuracy:
  • Review the human transcript carefully.
  • Always listen to the audio at each flagged error — clicking the error seeks the player to that spot, so you can hear the speaker's actual pronunciation. This is essential for Annunciation, Homonym/Rhyming, and Nonsense errors, which can't be judged from text alone.
  • Consider clinical context for severity rating
  • Check ALL applicable taxonomy categories
  • Be consistent in severity scoring
  • Take breaks every hour to avoid fatigue

8. FAQ

Q: Can an error have multiple categories?

A: YES! Select all applicable categories. Many errors span multiple dimensions.

Q: What if I make a mistake?

A: Click the error again to re-open and modify your annotation.

Q: How is my progress saved?

A: Annotations auto-save to the server after each save. Your progress persists across sessions.
